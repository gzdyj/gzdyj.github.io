# 2025 Year in Review

Memory is like a glass of water, time like an open faucet — the water in the glass grows fainter with each passing day. Before I knew it, it was the end of 2025 again. I'd planned a mid-year review back in 2024, but procrastination got the better of me. Life settled into a comfort zone — work consumed most of my days under fluorescent office lights, blurring the line between day and night. What little free time I had, I wanted to spend outdoors with friends. Today is the fourth day of my sick leave from the flu, and I hope to capture some memorable and meaningful moments before the clarity that illness brings is washed away by the joy of recovery.

## Work

Work this year was steady — methodical, rigorous, improving technically, and striving for excellence. Compared to last year, the biggest change has been how AI tools have transformed coding. With major improvements in context awareness, agent capabilities, and automated search, the hottest trend right now is **vibe coding** — completing coding, debugging, and testing entirely through natural language conversations. This has freed up a lot of mental bandwidth, letting AI handle repetitive, predictable tasks perfectly. On the backend, complex logic still needs careful checking, but on the frontend, simply articulating your ideas clearly can often yield perfect results from AI in one pass.

While AI has dramatically boosted productivity, new tools always bring disruption. AI has leveled the playing field for experience to a large extent, without creating many new job opportunities. As an ordinary person riding this wave, the best approach is probably to embrace change as much as possible and build a financial cushion for the uncertainties ahead.

## Learning

This year's learning was decent — I passed four exams in my self-study program. Not that I expect these credentials to be particularly useful, but they give me tangible milestones on my lifelong learning journey. Through exam preparation, I systematically revisited modern Chinese history and Marxist principles. While I only scratched the surface, I gained a lot. Marxist philosophy taught me to look at problems from a more abstract, higher-dimensional perspective, and modern Chinese history gave me a fresh understanding of the timeline and the role of key conferences.

I also learned about personal finance and savings, and my savings goals for the year are on track. My investments had ups and downs, but overall I'm in the green — though unrealized gains are just paper profits.

On learning methods: for unfamiliar domains, I now start with videos and beginner-friendly articles, saving books for in-depth reference and gap-filling. I try to apply the principle of negation of negation when encountering differing viewpoints.

## Health

Exercise this year was a mess — otherwise I wouldn't have caught colds repeatedly within a month. After getting two impacted wisdom teeth pulled during summer break, I completely gave up on working out. My annual checkup showed various minor abnormalities — partly from poor diet, partly from staying up late. Living alone gives too much freedom, and the combination of constant air conditioning, lack of outdoor activity, and late-night doom-scrolling took its toll.

## Books

### Money Money Money (Kira and the Kurrawong Club)

> When you set a big goal, you must put in much more effort than others.

A children's book, but an excellent introduction for anyone completely new to personal finance. Following the methods in this book, I completed my savings plan two months ahead of schedule.

### You Are What You Eat 2

> Don't be extreme in your diet — eat a diverse mix of everything.

This book reinforced my belief that matter determines consciousness and consciousness acts on matter. I've been gaining weight since starting work, mainly because of prolonged sitting, slow metabolism, and the habit of treating myself after work. Lack of sunlight in the office leads to vitamin D deficiency and a weakened immune system.

### MongoDB: Advanced Practices

> MongoDB's indexing, storage engine, concurrency (MVCC), locking mechanism, and oplog design share many principles with MySQL.

You never miss knowledge until you need it. Taking time to fill gaps in my understanding made working with related documentation much smoother.

### Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance

> When you're in a hurry to get something done, it means you no longer care about it and want to do something else.

The book is about a motorcycle road trip and repairs, but the philosophy applies to all technical work. A deeply insightful read.

### A Short History of Chinese Philosophy

> One must first speak many words, then remain silent.

I picked this up when I had ideological conflicts with my parents, hoping to understand why they think the way they do. Many of their ideas are deeply ingrained in their upbringing, and this book helped me connect the dots.

## Games

**Aokana: Four Rhythm Across the Blue**: Top-tier story and music across the board. All four heroines are exceptionally well-written. Appreciating art like this is a genuine pleasure.

**Honor of Kings**: I play a couple of matches every month or two as a lunch-break pastime. Ranking up too high brings out the try-hard mentality.

**Delta Force: Hawk Ops**: About 90 hours played. A great way to decompress on days off. No pay-to-win pressure, and bot matches make it easy to pick up.

At the end of the day, gaming is best when shared with friends.

## Travel

The more you go out, the more you suffer along the way — but the novelty you encounter on the road is something you'll never experience close to home.

**Mount Lu, Jiangxi**: I went during the off-season when it was free. The sun was warm at the foot of the mountain, but the summit was covered in snow. The ice on the steps forced us to walk on tiptoe. Unfortunately, the trip was rushed — after climbing the Five Old Men Peak, we were too exhausted to reach the Three Fold Spring.

**Changsha, Hunan**: I took an overnight green-train after work. The smell of feet and smoke on the train is still unforgettable. My second visit to Changsha opened a whole new door — I experienced what nightlife really means: 24-hour hotpot restaurants, 24-hour Mixue ice cream shops. This time I made it to the top of Yuelu Mountain, which I'd missed before. The mountain breeze and the panoramic view of downtown Changsha were unmatched.

**Tiantangzhai (Heavenly Village), Anhui**: I went during National Day. On the way up, the road disappeared into thick clouds with terrible visibility and rain. Then, after passing through a tunnel, the sky suddenly cleared. The walkways were built along cliff faces, swaying with the crowds — quite scary. The air at the summit was the freshest I've ever breathed. The cable car ride down was a bit too thrilling for someone who'd just read about cable car mechanics two days earlier.
